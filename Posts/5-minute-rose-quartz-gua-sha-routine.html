5-Minute Rose Quartz Gua Sha Routine for a Fresh, Glowing Face

A simple facial massage ritual for relaxation, gentle drainage and a more refreshed-looking complexion.

A rose quartz gua sha massage can become a calm and enjoyable addition to your skincare routine. When performed gently, it may help release facial tension, reduce the appearance of temporary puffiness and leave the face looking more rested and refreshed.

You do not need a long or complicated routine. The following five-minute method moves from the neck toward the upper face and finishes with gentle downward strokes toward the collarbones.

The goal is not to scrape, stretch or force the skin. Your movements should feel slow, smooth and comfortable.

What You Will Need

  • A clean rose quartz gua sha tool
  • Clean hands
  • A freshly cleansed face
  • Three to five drops of facial oil, serum or a moisturiser with enough slip
  • A mirror, especially while learning the technique

Before You Begin

Apply enough skincare product to allow the stone to glide smoothly across the face. The gua sha should never drag or pull the skin.

Hold the stone almost flat against the face, at a low angle, rather than pressing the edge perpendicularly into the skin.

Use light to moderate pressure on the cheeks and jawline, and extremely light pressure around the eyes. Every movement should remain pleasant and pain-free.

Your 5-Minute Rose Quartz Gua Sha Routine

0:00–0:40 — Neck Preparation and Gentle Drainage

Always begin with the neck. This prepares the area before you start massaging the face.

  1. Place the stone just below the ear.
  2. Glide gently down the side of the neck toward the collarbone.
  3. Repeat five times on each side.
  4. Then move from the centre area of the neck outward toward the sides.

Avoid strong pressure over the front of the throat. Keep the movement gentle and controlled.

0:40–1:30 — Jawline Massage

Use the curved or notched side of the gua sha tool.

  1. Begin at the centre of the chin.
  2. Follow the jawline slowly toward the ear.
  3. Repeat five to seven times on each side.
  4. At the ear, pause gently for one to two seconds.
  5. Finish by gliding down the side of the neck toward the collarbone.

Keep the teeth slightly apart and avoid clenching the jaw during the movement.

1:30–2:15 — Cheeks

Massage the cheeks using three gentle pathways.

  1. Glide from beside the nose outward toward the ear.
  2. Work along the area beneath the cheekbone.
  3. Glide across the cheekbone toward the temple.
  4. Move from the corner of the mouth outward toward the ear.
  5. Repeat each pathway four to five times on each side.

You may use slightly more pressure on the cheeks than around the eyes, but the movement should never hurt.

2:15–3:00 — Eye Area

The skin around the eyes is delicate. Use the smallest edge of the stone and extremely light pressure.

  1. Begin near the inner corner of the eye.
  2. Glide along the orbital bone beneath the eye toward the temple.
  3. Repeat four times on each side.
  4. Then move from beneath the eyebrow outward toward the temple.

Do not press directly over the eyeball and do not stretch the eyelid skin.

3:00–3:45 — Forehead

  1. Begin at the centre of the forehead.
  2. Glide outward toward the temples.
  3. Then move upward from the eyebrows toward the hairline.
  4. Repeat each movement four to five times.

For tension between the eyebrows, glide slowly upward using gentle pressure. Avoid repeatedly scraping or pressing firmly over the same area.

3:45–4:30 — Jaw Relaxation

The masseter muscles, located near the angle of the jaw, often hold tension from stress or clenching.

  1. Open the mouth slightly and keep the jaw relaxed.
  2. Place the stone over the masseter area.
  3. Use short, slow movements from the area beside the mouth toward the ear.
  4. Repeat five times on each side.

Pressure should feel soothing rather than painful. Stop if you notice jaw-joint pain, clicking or discomfort.

4:30–5:00 — Final Drainage

  1. Glide from the centre of the face outward toward the ears.
  2. Place the stone behind or just below the ears.
  3. Move slowly down the sides of the neck toward the collarbones.
  4. Repeat three times on each side.

Finish by lowering the shoulders, relaxing the jaw and taking one slow breath.

How Often Should You Use Gua Sha?

You may perform this routine three to five times per week, provided your skin remains comfortable and free from irritation.

A morning massage can be especially enjoyable when the face appears temporarily puffy after sleep. An evening massage may help release tension accumulated around the jaw, temples and forehead.

Consistency and gentle technique are more important than pressure or frequency.

Common Gua Sha Mistakes

  • Using the stone on dry skin
  • Holding the tool perpendicular to the face
  • Using painful or aggressive pressure
  • Dragging or stretching the skin
  • Moving too quickly
  • Pressing directly over the eyeballs
  • Massaging inflamed or irritated skin
  • Forgetting to clean the tool after use

When to Avoid Facial Gua Sha

Avoid massaging directly over:

  • Inflamed or painful acne lesions
  • Open wounds or broken skin
  • Active infections
  • Sunburn
  • Severe irritation or an active skin reaction
  • Unexplained swelling or pain

Do not perform facial gua sha immediately after injections, fillers, surgery, dental treatment or another cosmetic procedure unless the qualified practitioner responsible for your treatment has confirmed that massage is safe.

People taking blood-thinning medication, those who bruise easily or anyone with a medical condition affecting the skin, circulation, lymphatic system, face or neck should consult a qualified healthcare professional before beginning.

How to Clean Your Rose Quartz Tool

Wash the stone after every use with lukewarm water and a small amount of gentle soap. Rinse it thoroughly and dry it with a clean, soft towel.

Store it in a clean, dry place and avoid dropping it, as natural stone tools can crack or break.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should gua sha leave marks on the face?

A gentle facial gua sha routine should not create painful redness, bruising or lasting marks. Temporary mild flushing may occur, but reduce your pressure if the skin becomes sore or irritated.

Can I use gua sha every day?

Some people tolerate gentle daily massage, but more is not always better. Begin with three sessions per week and adjust according to your skin’s response.

Does the stone need to be cold?

No. Cooling the stone is optional. A cool tool may feel refreshing, but it does not need to be refrigerated for the routine to be effective as a relaxing facial massage.

Can I use moisturiser instead of facial oil?

Yes, provided the product offers enough slip for the stone to move without dragging the skin.

How much pressure should I use?

Use light pressure around the eyes and light to moderate pressure over the cheeks, jawline and forehead. The massage should never feel painful.

A Gentle Final Reminder

Gua sha is not about forcing the face into a different shape. It is a mindful massage practice that can help you slow down, release tension and care for your skin with greater awareness.

Keep the stone flat, use enough skincare product and allow every movement to feel smooth and unhurried. Five gentle minutes are enough.
